Notes on Rhetorical Analysis

Rhetorical analysis explains how a non-literary text makes an argument. The first step is to identify the rhetorical situation: speaker, audience, message, context, and purpose. The second step is to explain how the text’s choices respond to that situation.

Ethos, pathos, and logos describe appeals to credibility, emotion, and reasoning. They are not substitutes for analysis. I need to show where an appeal appears, what language or visual choice creates it, and how the intended audience is likely to respond.

Paragraph method

  1. Point: state the choice and the part of the argument it supports.
  2. Evidence: identify a specific word, image, structure, or design feature.
  3. Technique: name the relevant device only when the name clarifies the explanation.
  4. Analysis: explain the reader effect and connect it to purpose.
  5. Link: return to the text’s larger message or rhetorical situation.

This method keeps a paragraph from becoming a device list. The main question is always how the choice helps the text communicate with its audience.
