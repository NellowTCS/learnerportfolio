# Slaughterhouse-Five

_"So it goes."_

## Quick Facts

- **Author:** Kurt Vonnegut
- **Genre:** Novel (satire / science fiction)
- **Published:** 1969

## Notes

The novel uses satire, science fiction, nonlinear structure, and repeated
phrases to represent the effects of war and trauma. Billy Pilgrim's movement
through time prevents the story from presenting violence as a simple sequence
with a clean resolution. Instead, the structure shows how memory can return
without following ordinary chronology.

The repeated phrase "So it goes" creates a controlled, almost mechanical
response to death. Its repetition can sound accepting, numb, or critical,
depending on the surrounding event. This is why the phrase should be analyzed
in context rather than treated as a single definition. It means something
different next to a dead soldier than it does next to a dead dog.

For future notes, I am organizing passages by:

- the disruption of chronology and memory
- satire as a response to war and official narratives
- the contrast between ordinary detail and catastrophic violence
- Billy's limited perspective and the reader's distance from it
